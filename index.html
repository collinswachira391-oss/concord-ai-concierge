Interactive concept

The digital receptionist, without the waiting room.

A conversational layer can answer routine navigation questions, collect the information needed for an enquiry and escalate appropriately. Clinical diagnosis remains with qualified healthcare professionals.

Patient
“I need a blood test.”

Assistant identifies the laboratory journey, shows available guidance and routes the patient to booking/quote.

Patient
“I use SHA.”

Assistant explains the available SHA/SHIF pathway and what the patient should bring, then offers the next action.

Patient
“I need help now.”

Assistant surfaces emergency guidance and directs the person to immediate human/clinical support rather than attempting diagnosis.

Public-site audit · September 2026

What makes TFMC different.

The public site describes TFMC as a Nairobi SHA/SHIF-accredited family clinic with OPD, laboratory, pharmacy, ANC, chronic disease management, mental health, eye, dental and emergency services. It also promotes a 120+ condition health library, telemedicine, M-Pesa payments, patient resources, chronic-care clubs and community/school programmes. citeturn0search0

01 · The breadth is the challengeThere are many patient entry points. A smart navigation layer can turn a long list of services into a simple “what do you need today?” experience.
02 · Health education is an assetThe 120+ condition library creates a strong discovery channel. The assistant can connect educational content to appropriate, non-diagnostic next steps.
03 · Follow-up can become a productTFMC already describes WhatsApp reminders for chronic-care clubs and appointment/telemedicine flows. A unified follow-up layer could make those journeys easier to manage.
04 · Community growth is built inSchool health programmes, screenings, memberships and community initiatives provide additional journeys beyond ordinary clinic visits. citeturn0search0
